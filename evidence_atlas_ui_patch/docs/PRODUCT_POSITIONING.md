# Evidence Atlas — Product Design Principles

## Category

A paediatric oncology evidence-intelligence layer: a research environment that connects clinical context, molecular observations and experimental evidence while preserving source lineage, evidence semantics and uncertainty.

## Visual system

- Light-first scientific canvas: porcelain / cool grey.
- Deep ink-navy navigation and typography.
- Teal for observed data.
- Blue for curated mappings.
- Violet for derived transformations.
- Amber for inference/provisional interpretation.
- Red for conflicts or failed validation.
- Grey for missing or unassessed evidence.
- All semantic colours are paired with text labels.

## Product principles

1. Source-first: every visible evidence item can be traced to an input source and version.
2. Context-aware: paediatric patient evidence is not silently blended with adult-derived cell-line evidence.
3. Non-causal by default: exact gene-symbol overlap is not treated as a biological mechanism.
4. Uncertainty-preserving: missing/conflicting evidence is visible; no composite confidence score is invented.
5. Reproducible: future workspaces should preserve data versions, query parameters, transformations and outputs.
6. Honest capability states: built, validated, in development and not established are distinct.

## Strategic differentiation hypothesis

The potential advantage is not raw dataset volume, a generic knowledge graph or an AI score. It is the combination of paediatric-specific biological context, fine-grained evidence lineage, harmonised identifiers, explicit evidence semantics, reproducible investigations and feedback from expert research users.

This remains a hypothesis to demonstrate through partnerships, independent scientific review and adoption. Public-source integration alone does not create exclusive data rights or a validated clinical product.
